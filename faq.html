Understanding Seltzers

An FMB stands for "Flavored Malt Beverage" and many of the most popular spiked seltzers are considered FMBs. These are made with a fermenting process similar to traditional beer.

The difference between a vodka seltzer and an FMB is the alcohol. FMBs are fermented similar to beer, and vodka seltzers use vodka as the base alcohol. This can also be done with other alcohol bases as well.

Yes, there are many Keto Friendly spiked seltzers. Many Spiked Seltzers have 0 sugar added which makes them low in carbohydrates. There are also Spiked Seltzers that have as little as 70 calories per can.

Yes, there are many gluten free spiked seltzers on the market today. Though not all spiked seltzers are gluten free, many of the top brands such as White Claw and Truly are gluten free. Also, many seltzers made with real spirits such as High Noon are also gluten free.

The Seltzer Review rankings list is a comprehensive ranked database of every spiked seltzer, including ABV, calories, sugar, beach score, photos, video reviews, where it was made, and base alcohol type. It's an attempt to categorically rank every spiked seltzer on planet Earth (and beyond) for the betterment of mankind.

The spiked seltzers on the list are ranked by using a specific, extremely serious, scientific process developed by Matt. Seltzers are ranked using a multi-factor process:

  1. Smell, taste, mouth feel, carbonation, aesthetics, and even the sound of the crack.
  2. After a 90% of the can is consumed: Matt will consider the alcohol type, ABV, sugar content, calories, and terroir.
  3. Other flavors made by the same company/producer are then considered.
  4. "Beach score", snapshot, brief review with notes.
  5. Short video review followed by finishing the remaining 10% of the Seltzer.

The beach score represents the number of drinks (cans) one person could consume in a sitting before getting tired of the flavor. The Beach Score is one of the most important metrics on the Spiked Seltzer Reviews list. Colloquially, this represents the number of drinks you would like to have while enjoying a beautiful, sunny day at the beach. It's a scientific hypothesis proven out through rigorous experiments performed in real world trials.
